# Great Spectations, worked example

Generated by `examples/generate.py` (via `make examples`) from real `greatspectate` output against the files in this directory -- don't hand-edit this file, it will be overwritten.

The "spec" here is the famous opening paragraph of Dickens' *A Tale of Two Cities* (public domain) -- chosen because it has nothing to do with software, which is the point: `greatspectate` checks a quote against *any* text source, not just RFCs and protocol specs. All three source files below share one coverage file (`--coverage .coverage`), built up across the three `check` runs.

## The spec

```markdown
<!-- Public domain: Charles Dickens, "A Tale of Two Cities" (1859).
     Text from Project Gutenberg EBook #98 (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/98).
     Line-wrapped one clause per line (Gutenberg's own wrapping runs
     several clauses together per line, which -- fittingly for a demo
     of coverage tooling -- makes individual clauses impossible to
     tell apart at line granularity). -->

# Book the First -- Chapter I: The Period

It was the best of times, it was the worst of times,
it was the age of wisdom, it was the age of foolishness,
it was the epoch of belief, it was the epoch of incredulity,
it was the season of Light, it was the season of Darkness,
it was the spring of hope, it was the winter of despair,
we had everything before us, we had nothing before us,
we were all going direct to Heaven, we were all going direct the other way--
in short, the period was so far like the present period, that some of
its noisiest authorities insisted on its being received, for good or
for evil, in the superlative degree of comparison only.
```
